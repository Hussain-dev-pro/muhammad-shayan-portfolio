QuickBooks Online to Odoo: a Midyear Migration

Switching systems mid-year without losing open items or year-to-date reporting. Chart of accounts mapping, cutover date, open invoices and bills, year-to-date balances without double counting, and a cutover reconciliation.

Problem, solution and result

Problem

A midyear migration had to preserve unpaid customer invoices, outstanding supplier bills and year-to-date reporting. Incomplete or duplicated cutover balances would lose track of what customers owed and what the business had to pay.

Solution

Mapped the QuickBooks Online chart of accounts to Odoo and agreed a cutover date. Imported open invoices and bills, loaded remaining balances without double counting, compared trial balance and ageing with QuickBooks, and tested a payment against a pre-cutover invoice.

Result

Finance continues invoicing in Odoo, matches payments to pre-cutover invoices and sees which bills are due. The cutover reconciliation ties Odoo's opening balances back to QuickBooks.

What was going wrong

  • Unpaid customer invoices had to survive the switch so later payments could be matched.
  • Outstanding supplier bills had to carry over with their due dates.
  • Year-to-date and month-by-month reporting still had to work, with no balance counted twice.

Highlights from the engagement

  • QuickBooks Online chart of accounts mapped to Odoo account types.
  • Cutover date agreed; QuickBooks closed and frozen.
  • Open invoices and bills imported one by one into dedicated migration journals.
  • Prior year-end balances plus monthly movements loaded so year-to-date reporting continues in Odoo.
  • Trial balance, monthly profit and loss and ageing compared with QuickBooks; payment tested against a pre-cutover invoice.

Executive summary

The client, a US business, moved its accounting from QuickBooks Online to Odoo 17 Enterprise in the middle of its financial year. A midyear move is harder than a year-end move, because unpaid customer invoices, outstanding supplier bills and year-to-date reporting all have to survive the switch.

The main risk is in the balances at the switch. If receivables or payables are loaded twice (once as open documents and again inside the opening balances), or if some are missed, the finance team loses track of what customers owe and what the business needs to pay.

The migration mapped every QuickBooks account to an Odoo account with the right type, agreed and froze a month-end cutover date, imported open invoices and bills one by one into dedicated migration journals, loaded the prior year-end balance sheet plus monthly movements through a migration clearing account, and compared the results with QuickBooks before sign-off.
